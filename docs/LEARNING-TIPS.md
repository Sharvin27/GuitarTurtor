# Guitar learning tips, ranked, and what we add to the app

Compiled from guitar-teaching sites and magazines (links at the end). They are practitioner advice, not controlled studies, but the core points agree across sources. Ranked by how much they help a learner and how well the app can support them.

## Ranked tips

### 1. Practise slowly, then speed up in small steps (highest impact)
- Pick a tempo where you play the passage cleanly, and only raise it when it is clean. Increments of about 5 BPM are suggested; beginners often start metronome work around 50–60 BPM.
- Slow practice is described as the fastest route to real speed.
- **App support:** speed control on both pages; the video and the synth keep pitch when slowed down.

### 2. Deliberate practice: one specific weakness at a time
- Choose the bar or transition you get wrong, work it at the edge of your ability and fix mistakes as they happen. Playing a song straight through for fun does not count as practice.
- **App support:** bar looping, sections, the "Lesson cam" on one part of the neck.

### 3. Chunking: learn 2–4 notes or one bar at a time
- Join chunks only after each is clean. Many professionals learn this way.
- **App support:** loop one bar, then two, then a section.

### 4. Short, frequent sessions
- Around 30 minutes of focused practice most days beats one long weekly session. Take a short break every 20–30 minutes.
- **App support:** (planned) a gentle break reminder.

### 5. Finger independence and the weak fingers
- The ring finger is the most naturally restricted. A spider exercise (1-2-3-4 across all six strings, one finger per fret) builds independence.
- Mute strings you are not playing, and keep fingers close to the fretboard.
- **App support:** finger colours in the tab and 3D view show which finger plays each note.

### 6. Hammer-ons, pull-offs and slides: slow and even
- Pick only the first note, then slur the rest. Make every note equally loud.
- Hammer-on: strike down firmly just behind the fret. Pull-off: pull slightly sideways off the string so the lower note sounds. Slide: keep pressure while you glide.
- **App support:** H, P and slide markers on the strings and in the tab; slow speeds.

### 7. Bending in tune
- Pitch centring is the key skill. Bend with the wrist, not only the fingers, and hook the thumb over the neck for leverage.
- Ear-check: play the target note, then bend and match it, or bend one string to match a higher string (the pulsing between them stops when they agree).
- Pre-bend silently, then pick; add vibrato once the bend is at pitch.
- **App support:** bend arrows (full or half), held bend and release symbols, target fret shown.

### 8. Play along, then play without the support
- Play with the recording, then with a metronome only, then from memory.
- **App support:** original video or synth, speed control, Hide UI view for recording yourself.

### 9. Look after your hands
- Warm up 5–10 minutes, relax shoulders, feet flat, back upright, stretch without pain, and stop if anything hurts. Raise playing time gradually.

### 10. Hear it before you play it (ear training)
- Sing or hum the note, find it by ear, check it against the tab.
